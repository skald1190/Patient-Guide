# Rules for everyone

## A1: RULES FOR EVERY MEDICINE

Everyone should read this part, no matter which condition or medicine you have.

☐ **Start low, go slow.** Most of these medicines start at a small dose and go up step by step. This helps your body get used to them. Follow your step-up calendar exactly. **Never go up faster than written**, even if you feel fine.

☐ **Side effects are often worst in the first 1 to 2 weeks** and then fade. If a side effect is mild, give it time. If it is hard to live with, stay at the dose you are on (do not go up) and call us.

☐ **Missed a dose?** Take it when you remember, unless it is almost time for the next dose. **Never take a double dose.** (Some medicines have a different rule. It is written in their section.)

☐ **Do not stop suddenly** unless this guide or your doctor tells you to. Many of these medicines need to be lowered slowly.

☐ **New medicine from another doctor?** Ask your pharmacist to check it against your neurology medicines. This includes antibiotics, cold medicines, birth control, and supplements. **Read A3 (interaction alerts).**

☐ **Alcohol, sleep aids, pain pills, and cannabis** can add to sleepiness and dizziness with many of these medicines. Avoid them or ask first.

☐ **Do not drive** until you know how a new medicine affects you.

☐ **Pregnant, planning pregnancy, or breastfeeding?** Every medicine in this guide has a pregnancy and breastfeeding label. **Read A2.** Tell us before starting or stopping any of these medicines.

**When to call us vs. when to get emergency help**

☐ **Call our office** for: side effects that do not go away, questions about your step-up calendar, or symptoms that are not improving.

☐ **Call 911 or go to the ER** for: trouble breathing or swallowing, swelling of the face, lips, or tongue, a seizure that will not stop, chest pain, fainting, a sudden severe headache, or new weakness, numbness, or trouble speaking.

## A2: PREGNANCY AND BREASTFEEDING: HOW TO READ THE LABELS

Every medicine in this guide has a line like this:

**Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

| **Label**              | **What it means**                                                                            | **What you should do**                                                                                                                                 |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ✅ **Generally OK**    | Research so far is reassuring. Most people can keep taking it.                               | Keep taking it. Tell us you are pregnant or breastfeeding so we can check your dose.                                                                   |
| ⚠️ **Discuss with us** | There is not enough research, or there are some risks to weigh against the benefits for you. | **Do not stop on your own.** Call us. Together we will decide whether to continue, lower, or switch.                                                   |
| ⛔ **Avoid**           | It can harm a pregnancy or a nursing baby, or it is not recommended.                         | Use reliable birth control. **Talk to us before you try to get pregnant.** Some medicines must be stopped weeks to months ahead (see the table below). |

**Important:**

- **If you find out you are pregnant, do not stop any medicine on your own, especially seizure medicines.** Stopping suddenly can be more dangerous for you and your baby than the medicine. Call us the same day.

- Your condition matters too. For example, uncontrolled seizures, myasthenia, or MS attacks can be risky during pregnancy. The goal is the safest plan for **both** of you.

- **Plan ahead.** The best time to talk about pregnancy is **before** you try. We may switch medicines, adjust doses, or start folic acid first.

## A3: MEDICINE INTERACTION ALERTS

Some medicines change how other medicines work. These are the most important ones in neurology. **Show this page to any doctor who prescribes you something new.**

**☐ Alert 1: Birth control (estrogen pills, patches, and rings)**

**These medicines can make birth control pills, patches, rings, the implant (Nexplanon), and progestin-only pills work less well:**

- Carbamazepine (Tegretol)

- Oxcarbazepine (Trileptal)

- Phenytoin (Dilantin)

- Phenobarbital

- Primidone (Mysoline)

- Cenobamate (Xcopri)

- Topiramate (Topamax), mostly at doses above 200 mg a day

- Modafinil (Provigil)

- Mycophenolate (CellCept)

**If you take one of these, the most reliable birth control choices are:**

- An IUD: hormonal (such as Mirena) or copper (Paragard). These are not affected.

- The birth control shot (Depo-Provera). Your gynecologist may give it a little more often.

**If you want to stay on the pill:** talk to your gynecologist. They may use a higher-estrogen pill, skip the placebo (sugar pill) week, and add condoms as a backup.

**Estrogen lowers lamotrigine (Lamictal) levels.** Birth control with estrogen can lower your lamotrigine level by about half. This can cause breakthrough seizures. **Tell us before starting or stopping estrogen birth control** so we can adjust your dose. Progestin-only methods and IUDs do not affect lamotrigine much. See the lamotrigine section (Section 5) for details.

**☐ Alert 2: Phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone (and carbamazepine)**

These older seizure medicines speed up how your liver clears many other medicines. **The other medicines can stop working as well.** Tell every doctor you take one of these.

| **Medicines that may work less well**                                                                   | **What to do**                                                                                              |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Blood thinners:** Eliquis (apixaban), Xarelto (rivaroxaban), Pradaxa (dabigatran), Savaysa (edoxaban) | **Usually should not be taken together.** Your doctors may choose warfarin or a different seizure medicine. |
| **Warfarin (Coumadin)**                                                                                 | Needs extra INR blood checks when starting, stopping, or changing doses.                                    |
| **Birth control**                                                                                       | See Alert 1.                                                                                                |
| **Steroids** (prednisone, dexamethasone)                                                                | May need a higher dose.                                                                                     |
| **Other seizure medicines** (lamotrigine, topiramate, lacosamide, oxcarbazepine)                        | Doses may need to change.                                                                                   |
| **Heart and blood pressure medicines** (verapamil, nimodipine, some cholesterol pills)                  | May work less well.                                                                                         |
| **Mood and sleep medicines** (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, bupropion, quetiapine)                      | May work less well.                                                                                         |
| **Transplant medicines** (tacrolimus, cyclosporine)                                                     | Levels must be checked.                                                                                     |
| **Vitamin D**                                                                                           | Levels drop over time. Ask about vitamin D and bone health.                                                 |

**Also good to know:**

- **Folic acid** can lower phenytoin levels a little. Keep taking folic acid, but tell us so we can check your level.

- **When one of these is started or stopped, other medicine doses may need to change for several weeks.** Never start or stop them on your own.

**☐ Alert 3: Bupropion (Wellbutrin) with duloxetine (Cymbalta)**

- Bupropion **raises duloxetine levels** in your blood. This can cause more nausea, dizziness, sweating, or a racing heart.

- Bupropion can also **raise seizure risk**, especially at high doses.

- Taking both together can be OK, but **tell us** so we can adjust your doses and watch for side effects.

- **Call us right away** for fever, shaking, sweating, confusion, or a fast heartbeat. These can be signs of serotonin syndrome, a rare reaction.

**☐ Other common interactions** are listed in each medicine's section under "Avoid" or "Good to know."
