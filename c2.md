# Your immune medicine

Read only the sections for the medicines you take.

### If you have NMOSD or MOGAD, which sections apply to you?

These are conditions where the immune system attacks the optic nerves (the nerves to the eyes), the spinal cord, and sometimes the brain. The goal of treatment is to **prevent attacks**, because each attack can leave lasting damage. Most people stay on treatment long term. **Do not stop or space out your medicine on your own**, even if you feel well. Stopping can trigger a serious attack.

## Prednisone (steroid) {#prednisone}

**How to take it:** Once every morning with food. **Never stop suddenly.** Your body gets used to steroids. Stopping suddenly can cause a dangerous drop in your body's own steroid hormone (cortisol).

**Your dose schedule.** We will check the plan that applies to you.

#### Myasthenia gravis: slow increase, then slow decrease {choice when=mg}

1.  Start at 10 mg a day. Increase by 5 mg each week until your symptoms are controlled.

2.  Stay at that dose for 1 month.

3.  Then lower your dose:

- Above 30 mg: lower by 10 mg every month

- From 30 mg down to 10 mg: lower by 5 mg every month

- Below 10 mg: lower by 2.5 mg every month until you reach 5 mg

2.  **Warning:** myasthenia can get temporarily worse in the first 1 to 2 weeks of prednisone, often starting around day 5. Go to the emergency room if swallowing, speaking, or breathing gets worse.

3.  If symptoms come back while lowering, **message us. Do not change the dose on your own.** We usually go back up to the last dose that worked.

#### Myositis (muscle inflammation) {choice when=myositis}

1.  Full dose (about 1 mg per kg of body weight) for 4 to 6 weeks

2.  Lower by 10 mg every 4 weeks until you reach 20 mg

3.  Lower by 2.5 mg every 2 weeks until you reach 10 mg (around month 6)

4.  Lower by 2.5 mg every month until you reach 5 mg

#### Vasculitic neuropathy (nerve damage from inflamed blood vessels) {choice when=vasculitic}

1.  Full dose for 4 weeks

2.  Lower by 10 mg every 2 weeks until you reach 30 mg, then 25 mg by month 3

3.  Lower by 2.5 mg every 2 weeks until you reach 10 mg by month 6

4.  Stay on 7.5 mg from month 6 until month 12 to 18. This helps prevent a relapse.

5.  Then 5 mg

#### CIDP {choice when=cidp}

1.  60 mg a day for 4 weeks

2.  Lower by 10 mg every 4 weeks until you reach 20 mg (around month 5)

3.  Lower by 5 mg every 4 weeks until you reach 5 mg (around month 8)

#### NMOSD or MOGAD {choice when=nmosd}

1.  After an attack, prednisone is often lowered slowly over several months, sometimes while your long-term medicine starts working.

2.  **Lowering too fast can bring on a new attack**, especially in MOGAD. Follow your schedule exactly.

3.  My schedule: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

#### My dose right now

**My current dose:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ mg. **Next change on:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

**When you reach 5 mg:** we will check a morning cortisol blood test. This test shows whether your body is making enough of its own steroid hormone.

- Skip your prednisone for 24 hours before the test.

- Get blood drawn at about 8 AM.

- Take your prednisone right after the blood draw.

- We will tell you whether to stop, stay at 5 mg, or recheck later.

**If you get very sick, are vomiting and cannot keep prednisone down, or need surgery:** tell the doctors that you take prednisone. You may need extra steroid for a short time.

#### Protecting your body:

☐ **Calcium** 1000 mg plus **vitamin D3** 800 IU every day, to protect your bones  
☐ **Bone density scan (DXA)** within 6 months of starting, then every 2 years while on steroids  
☐ **HbA1c (blood sugar test)** every 3 months for the first year, then once a year  
☐ **Eye exam** once a year (steroids can cause cataracts and glaucoma)  
☐ **Blood pressure and weight** checked at each visit  
☐ **Pneumonia prevention:** see the antibiotic box below

**PJP pneumonia prevention.** While you take prednisone 20 mg a day or more, you will take an antibiotic to prevent a rare but serious lung infection called PJP (Pneumocystis pneumonia).

- **Usual choice:** Bactrim (sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim) single strength, 1 tablet once a day. We adjust the dose if your kidneys are weak.

- **If you are allergic to sulfa, or if you take methotrexate:** atovaquone 1500 mg (10 mL liquid) once a day with food.

- **Stop Bactrim and call us** if you get a rash.

- **Bactrim: Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (usually avoided in newborns and premature babies)

**Common side effects:** bigger appetite and weight gain, trouble sleeping, mood changes or irritability, higher blood sugar, higher blood pressure, easy bruising, and stomach upset.

**Call us for:** very high blood sugars, extreme thirst or urination, mood changes that worry you, vision changes, or new weakness in your thighs or shoulders (steroids themselves can weaken muscles).

**Interactions:** phenytoin, phenobarbital, primidone, and carbamazepine can make prednisone work less well. See **A3, Alert 2**.

**Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK (we use the lowest dose that controls your disease) · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK. **If your dose is 20 mg a day or more,** wait 4 hours after your dose before breastfeeding or pumping.

## Mycophenolate (CellCept) {#mycophenolate}

**What it does:** It lets us lower your prednisone over time. It is slow to work, often 6 months or longer. We plan your prednisone decrease around that timing.

#### How to take it:

- 500 mg twice a day for 2 weeks

- Then 1000 mg twice a day

#### Blood tests (CBC):

☐ Weekly for the first 4 weeks  
☐ Monthly for the first year  
☐ Then every 6 months

**Common side effects:** diarrhea, nausea, stomach cramps, and lower blood counts.

**Call us for:** fever, unusual bruising or bleeding, severe or ongoing diarrhea, or new confusion or weakness.

**Interactions:** take mycophenolate at a different time of day from antacids (Tums, Maalox, Mylanta), iron, and calcium supplements. These lower how much medicine your body absorbs.

**Skin:** get a skin cancer check every year.

**Pregnancy:** ⛔ **Avoid.** Mycophenolate causes birth defects and miscarriage. You need a pregnancy test before starting and reliable birth control while you take it. It can also make birth control pills work less well (see **A3, Alert 1**). **Stop at least 6 weeks before trying to get pregnant.** · **Breastfeeding:** ⛔ **Avoid**

## Methotrexate {#methotrexate}

⚠️ **Take methotrexate ONCE A WEEK only, on the same day each week.** Taking it every day by mistake can be life-threatening.

**My methotrexate day:** \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_

#### How to take it:

- Start at 15 mg once a week. After 4 weeks, increase to 20 mg once a week. The dose can go up to 25 mg once a week.

- If pills upset your stomach, we can switch you to a weekly shot.

- **Folic acid 1 mg every day.** It lowers side effects.

- **Limit alcohol.** Alcohol and methotrexate together raise the risk of liver damage.

#### Blood tests:

☐ CBC, liver tests, and kidney test (creatinine) every month

**PJP pneumonia prevention:** while you take prednisone 20 mg a day or more, take **atovaquone** 1500 mg (10 mL liquid) once a day with food. **Do not take Bactrim with methotrexate.** Together they can dangerously lower your blood counts.

**Interactions:** check with us before taking any of these:

- Bactrim or other sulfa antibiotics (avoid)

- Penicillin-type antibiotics

- Regular use of ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin), naproxen (Aleve), or high-dose aspirin

- Stomach acid medicines such as omeprazole (Prilosec)

**Common side effects:** nausea or tiredness the day after your dose, mouth sores, and hair thinning.

#### Call us for:

- A new cough or shortness of breath (rarely, methotrexate inflames the lungs)

- Fever

- Bad mouth sores

- Yellow skin or eyes, or dark urine

- Unusual bruising

**Pregnancy:** ⛔ **Avoid.** Methotrexate causes miscarriage and birth defects. Use reliable birth control. **Stop at least 3 months before trying to get pregnant.** If your partner is planning a pregnancy, talk to us too. · **Breastfeeding:** ⛔ **Avoid**

## Azathioprine (Imuran) {#azathioprine}

**Before starting:** a TPMT blood test. It shows how your body breaks down this medicine, so we can dose it safely.

#### How to take it:

- Start at 50 mg once a day.

- Increase by 50 mg every 2 weeks until you reach your target dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_ mg.

- If it upsets your stomach, you can split the dose into morning and evening.

- It takes 6 to 12 months to fully work.

#### Blood tests (CBC and liver tests):

☐ Weekly for the first 4 weeks  
☐ Monthly for the first year  
☐ Then every 3 months

#### Interactions:

- **Tell every doctor you take azathioprine before starting allopurinol (Zyloprim) or febuxostat (Uloric).** These gout medicines can cause dangerous drops in blood counts when taken with azathioprine.

- Bactrim can also lower blood counts. If you take both, your regular blood tests will check for this.

**Common side effects:** nausea and tiredness.

#### Call us for:

- Fever, aches, or flu-like illness in the first few weeks (this can be a reaction to the medicine)

- Bad belly pain, especially if it goes to your back (pancreatitis)

- Yellow skin or eyes

- Unusual bruising or bleeding

**Skin and cancer:** get a skin cancer check every year, use sunscreen, and keep up with routine cancer screenings.

**Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK (often continued through pregnancy) · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

## IVIG (intravenous immune globulin) {#ivig}

**What it is:** antibodies from healthy donors, given by IV. It calms the immune attack on your nerves, or on your optic nerves, brain, and spinal cord in MOGAD.

**MOGAD:** IVIG is one of the most common medicines used to prevent MOGAD attacks. It is usually given every 4 weeks. Tell us if you notice vision or other symptoms creeping back near the end of each 4-week cycle.

#### Schedule:

- **Loading dose:** given over 5 days

- **Then a maintenance dose every 4 weeks**, given over 2 days

- Once you are stable, we may slowly try lowering the dose or spacing out infusions, to see if you need less. If you get worse, we go back to the dose that worked.

#### On infusion days:

- **Drink extra fluids** the day before and the day of each infusion.

- If you had a reaction before, take your pre-medicines 1 hour before the infusion as directed: acetaminophen (Tylenol) 650 mg plus cetirizine (Zyrtec) 10 mg.

#### Blood tests:

☐ Kidney tests before each infusion for the first 3 months, then every 6 months  
☐ CBC every 6 months

**Common side effects:** headache, tiredness, flu-like feeling, and rash. These usually happen during or within 1 to 2 days of an infusion.

#### Go to the emergency room for:

- A severe headache with a stiff neck

- Chest pain, shortness of breath, or a swollen leg

- New weakness or trouble speaking

- Very little urine, or dark urine

**Track your symptoms.** Tell us if your symptoms come back before your next infusion is due. We may change your schedule.

**Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

## Subcutaneous immune globulin (Hizentra), home infusion {#scig}

**What it is:** the same treatment as IVIG, but given under the skin at home once a week. A home nurse will teach you how.

**Schedule:** start 1 week after your last IVIG dose, then once every week.

#### How to give it:

- Use the belly, thighs, upper arms, or sides of the hips.

- Use several spots at once, and change spots each week.

- Drink plenty of fluids.

#### Blood tests:

☐ Kidney tests and CBC every 6 to 12 months

**Common side effects:** swelling, redness, or itching where you infuse. This usually gets better after the first few weeks. Headache is less common than with IVIG.

**Go to the emergency room for:** chest pain, shortness of breath, a swollen leg, new weakness or trouble speaking, or a severe headache with a stiff neck.

**Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

## Rituximab (Rituxan, Truxima, Ruxience, Riabni) {#rituximab}

**What it does:** it removes B cells, the immune cells that make harmful antibodies. It is used for myasthenia, vasculitis, NMOSD, MOGAD, and other conditions.

#### Schedule:

- Two IV infusions, 2 weeks apart (day 1 and day 15)

- Usually repeated about every 6 months

- **NMOSD:** do not skip or delay your repeat dose. Attacks can return as B cells come back.

#### Infusion day:

- You will get pre-medicines (a steroid, Tylenol, and Benadryl) to prevent reactions.

- The first infusion takes the longest.

#### Blood tests:

☐ Before each dose: CBC, CMP, and IgG (antibody level)

**Vaccines:** if possible, get all vaccines **ideally 4 weeks (at least 2 weeks) before your first dose.** After that, get vaccines (except the flu shot) ideally 4 weeks (at least 2 weeks) before your next rituximab dose. They work best then. The flu shot can be given on its usual schedule. See Checklist 2 for hepatitis B.

**PJP pneumonia prevention:** Bactrim single strength, 1 tablet daily while on prednisone 20 mg a day or more. If you are treated for vasculitis, keep taking it until 6 months after your last rituximab dose.

#### Common side effects:

- Infusion reactions (fever, chills, itching, rash, dizziness), mostly with the first infusion. Tell the nurse right away.

- More frequent infections, such as colds, sinus infections, and urinary infections

#### Call us for:

- Frequent or hard-to-treat infections

- Yellow skin or eyes (hepatitis B can rarely wake up)

- New confusion, vision changes, or new weakness. These are rare signs of a serious brain infection called PML.

**Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us. Use birth control while on rituximab. **Talk to us before planning a pregnancy** so we can time your doses. If given later in pregnancy, your baby's B cells may be low for a few months. · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK (very little gets into breast milk)

## Cyclophosphamide (IV) {#cyclophosphamide}

**Schedule:** IV infusion every 2 to 4 weeks, depending on your condition, for several months. After that, we switch you to a pill-based medicine to keep your disease controlled.

#### Infusion day:

- Take your anti-nausea medicine before the infusion.

- **Drink at least 3 liters (about 12 cups) of fluid that day.**

- **Urinate often, including during the night.** This protects your bladder.

#### Blood and urine tests:

☐ CBC before each dose  
☐ CBC 10 to 14 days after each dose (when blood counts are lowest)  
☐ Urine test before each dose, then every 6 months after you finish

**PJP pneumonia prevention:** Bactrim single strength, 1 tablet daily for the whole treatment course. If you are allergic to sulfa, we will use atovaquone.

**Common side effects:** nausea, tiredness, hair thinning, and low blood counts.

#### Call us right away for:

- Fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher (your blood counts may be low)

- Blood in your urine

- Unusual bruising or bleeding

**Fertility:** cyclophosphamide can cause infertility in men and women. Ask us about fertility preservation before you start.

**Long-term:** there is a slightly higher risk of some cancers (bladder, blood, and skin). Keep up with your urine tests and routine cancer screenings.

**Pregnancy:** ⛔ **Avoid.** It causes birth defects. Reliable birth control is required. **Wait at least 3 months after the last dose before trying to get pregnant.** · **Breastfeeding:** ⛔ **Avoid**

## Complement inhibitors: Ultomiris (ravulizumab), Soliris (eculizumab), Zilbrysq (zilucoplan) {#complement-inhibitors}

- Fever

- Headache with a stiff neck, or sensitivity to light

- A rash

- Confusion

- Flu-like illness with vomiting

**Who uses them:** Ultomiris and Soliris are used for myasthenia gravis and for NMOSD (aquaporin-4 antibody positive). Zilbrysq is used for myasthenia gravis.

**Ultomiris schedule (myasthenia and NMOSD):** IV loading dose, then a dose 2 weeks later, then every 8 weeks. Common side effects are diarrhea, colds, and infusion reactions.

**Soliris schedule (NMOSD):** IV infusion once a week for 4 weeks, then a dose at week 5, then every 2 weeks. Common side effects are headache, colds, and infusion reactions.

**Zilbrysq schedule:** one shot under the skin every day. A pharmacy nurse will teach you how. Common side effects are bruising or redness where you inject, colds, and diarrhea.

- **Stop Zilbrysq and call us** for belly pain that won't go away, especially if it spreads to your back or comes with vomiting. This can be a sign of pancreatitis.

**Other:** these medicines also raise the risk of gonorrhea infection. Use protection, and get tested if you could be at risk.

- **Soliris: Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (sometimes continued during pregnancy when needed) · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK (not found in breast milk in studies)

- **Ultomiris: Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us

- **Zilbrysq: Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us

## FcRn medicines: Vyvgart Hytrulo (efgartigimod), Rystiggo (rozanolixizumab), Imaavy (nipocalimab) {#fcrn-medicines}

**What they do:** they quickly lower the antibodies that cause your symptoms. They also lower your normal antibody level (IgG), so you may be more likely to get infections.

#### For all three:

☐ IgG blood test before starting, and before new treatment cycles (or every 6 months if you are on ongoing treatment)  
☐ Your Checklist 2 vaccines (flu, COVID, Shingrix) before starting when possible. Ask your primary care doctor about any others.  
☐ No live vaccines during treatment  
☐ If you have an infection, call before your next dose. We may delay it.

#### Vyvgart Hytrulo:

- One shot under the skin, given over about 30 seconds. A pharmacy nurse will teach you.

- **Myasthenia gravis:** once a week for 4 weeks = 1 cycle. The next cycle starts when your symptoms return.

- **CIDP:** once a week, ongoing.

- **Missed dose:** give it within 3 days, then go back to your usual day.

- **Common side effects:** injection-site redness, colds, headache, and urinary infections.

#### Rystiggo:

- Infusion under the skin once a week for 6 weeks = 1 cycle. The next cycle starts when your symptoms return.

- **Missed dose:** give it within 4 days, then go back to your usual day.

- **Common side effects:** headache (can be strong), diarrhea, fever, and nausea.

- **Go to the emergency room** for a severe headache with fever, stiff neck, or sensitivity to light.

#### Imaavy:

- IV infusion every 2 weeks, ongoing (not in cycles).

- Cholesterol blood test at the start and at week 4, then from time to time. Cholesterol can rise on this medicine.

- **Common side effects:** colds, leg swelling, and muscle spasms.

- Allergic reactions can happen up to 2 weeks after a dose. Call us for a rash, hives, or swelling.

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding (all three):** these medicines may lower the protective antibodies that pass to a baby during pregnancy. There is little safety information.

- **Vyvgart Hytrulo, Rystiggo, Imaavy: Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us

## Fingolimod (Gilenya), MS {#fingolimod}

**Before starting:** blood tests, a chickenpox immunity test, an ECG (heart test), and an eye exam (OCT). If you are not immune to chickenpox, you will need the vaccine first, then start 1 month later.

**First dose:** given with 6 hours of monitoring. We check your heart rate and blood pressure every hour, because the first dose can slow your heart rate.

**How to take it:** 0.5 mg once a day.

⚠️ **If you miss doses, call us before restarting.** You may need heart monitoring again.

⚠️ **Do not stop fingolimod on your own.** MS can come back severely after stopping.

#### Tests:

☐ CBC and liver tests at 3 months, 6 months, then every 6 months  
☐ Eye exam (OCT) at 3 to 4 months, and any time your vision changes  
☐ Blood pressure at 1 month, then every year  
☐ Skin check every year

**Interactions:** tell us before starting any heart rhythm medicine or any medicine that slows the heart rate.

**Common side effects:** headache, higher blood pressure, and higher liver tests on blood work. Your lymphocyte count will be low on fingolimod; that is expected.

#### Call us for:

- Blurred or wavy vision

- Dizziness or fainting

- A shingles rash or cold sores that won't heal

- A bad headache with fever or stiff neck

- New weakness or confusion

**Live vaccines:** avoid during treatment and for 2 months after stopping.

**Pregnancy:** ⛔ **Avoid.** It can raise the risk of birth defects. Use birth control during treatment and for 2 months after stopping. **Talk to us well before trying to get pregnant.** We will switch you to a pregnancy-friendly medicine so your MS does not come back strongly. · **Breastfeeding:** ⛔ **Avoid**

## Mayzent (siponimod), MS {#mayzent}

**Before starting:** a genetic blood test (CYP2C9) to choose your dose, blood tests, a chickenpox immunity test, an ECG, and an eye exam. If you are not immune to chickenpox, you will need the vaccine first, then start Mayzent 1 month later.

**Starter schedule:** your starter pack increases the dose slowly over 5 to 6 days, which protects your heart. Swallow tablets whole.

## Tecfidera (dimethyl fumarate) and Vumerity (diroximel fumarate), MS {#tecfidera}

#### How to take it:

- **Tecfidera:** 120 mg twice a day for 7 days, then 240 mg twice a day. Take with food.

- **Vumerity:** 231 mg twice a day for 7 days, then 462 mg (2 capsules) twice a day. Avoid taking it with a high-fat meal or alcohol.

- Swallow capsules whole.

**Flushing tips:** take a regular (not coated) aspirin 325 mg 30 minutes before your dose. Flushing and stomach upset are common in the first month and usually get better.

#### Blood tests:

☐ CBC and liver tests every 6 months  
☐ If you switched from Tecfidera to Vumerity: CBC at 3 months after switching

**Why we watch your lymphocytes:** if they stay very low for a long time, the risk of a rare brain infection (PML) goes up. We may change your medicine if this happens.

#### Call us for:

- New weakness, vision changes, or confusion

- A shingles rash

- Yellow skin or eyes

**Go to the emergency room** for swelling of the face, lips, or throat.

**Vaccines:** non-live vaccines are fine any time. Live vaccines only if your lymphocyte count is high enough. Ask us first.

**Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (usually continued until a positive pregnancy test, then stopped) · **Breastfeeding:** ⛔ **Avoid** (not enough safety information)

## Copaxone / Glatopa (glatiramer acetate), MS {#copaxone-glatopa}

**How to take it:** one shot under the skin, either 20 mg every day or 40 mg 3 times a week (at least 48 hours apart). Change injection spots every time.

**Blood tests:** none needed routinely.

**Vaccines:** no restrictions. Live vaccines are allowed.

**Common side effects:** redness, pain, or lumps where you inject.

**Post-injection reaction:** flushing, chest tightness, racing heart, anxiety, or trouble breathing within minutes after a shot.

- This usually goes away on its own within minutes.

- It can happen months after starting.

- **Call 911** if it is severe or does not go away.

**Allergic reaction** can happen any time, even years into treatment. If it happens, stop the medicine and call us.

**Other:** fat loss under the skin (dents) can happen at injection spots, so rotate spots. Rarely, the liver is affected: call us for yellow skin, dark urine, or belly pain.

**Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK (can be continued while trying and often during pregnancy) · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK
