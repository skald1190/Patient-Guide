# Section 6: Parkinson's disease and movement disorders

### About these conditions

**Parkinson's disease** happens when brain cells that make **dopamine**, a chemical messenger that helps control movement, slowly stop working. This causes slowness, stiffness, tremor, and balance problems. Many people also have symptoms that are not about movement, such as constipation, low mood, anxiety, sleep problems, loss of smell, or dizziness when standing. The medicines below replace or boost dopamine. They work well for movement symptoms, but they do not stop the disease from slowly changing over time, so your doses will be adjusted over the years. Staying active, exercising, and physical therapy also help.

This section also covers medicine for **tardive dyskinesia** (repeated movements of the mouth, face, or body caused by certain medicines) and **chorea** (dance-like movements) from Huntington's disease.

Medicines in this section: carbidopa-levodopa (Sinemet, Crexont, Rytary), entacapone, opicapone, tolcapone, pramipexole, tavapadon, deutetrabenazine.

Also see: Section 14 (blood pressure drops), Section 15 (bladder), Section 7 (hallucinations), and Section 16 (sleep).

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** there is little safety information for most Parkinson's medicines. Dopamine medicines can **lower breast milk supply.** If you could become pregnant or are breastfeeding, talk to us.

### Rules for all Parkinson's medicines

- [ ] **Never stop suddenly.** This can cause a dangerous reaction with high fever and stiffness.

- [ ] **Sudden sleepiness:** you can fall asleep without warning. **Do not drive if you feel drowsy.**

- [ ] **New urges:** tell us about any new urges to gamble, shop, eat, or have sex. Family members often notice first. These go away when the dose is adjusted.

- [ ] **Tell us** about hallucinations, dizziness when standing, or extra movements (dyskinesia).

- [ ] **Skin:** get a yearly skin check. Parkinson's slightly raises melanoma risk.

## Carbidopa-levodopa (Sinemet), 25/100 mg tablets {#carbidopa-levodopa}

| **Week**      | **Breakfast time**                                           | **Lunch time** | **Dinner time** | **Done** |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| Week 1        | ½ tablet                                                     | ½ tablet       | ½ tablet        | ☐        |
| Week 2 and on | 1 tablet                                                     | 1 tablet       | 1 tablet        | ☐        |
| After that    | Stay here until your next visit unless we tell you otherwise |                |                 |          |

- **Timing:** take it **30 to 60 minutes before meals.** It works best on an empty stomach.

- **Nauseated?** Take it with a few crackers or a small non-protein snack.

- **Protein:** high-protein meals (meat, eggs, dairy, protein shakes) can block levodopa. **Do not cut out protein.** If your doses seem weaker after meals, try having more of your protein at dinner.

- **Iron pills and multivitamins with iron:** take them at a different time of day from levodopa.

- **Good to know:** urine, sweat, or saliva may turn dark. This is harmless but can stain clothes.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (can lower milk supply)

## Entacapone (Comtan) {#entacapone}

- **How to take:** 200 mg **with each** carbidopa-levodopa dose. No more than 8 a day. It does nothing on its own.

- **Side effects:** orange-brown urine (harmless), diarrhea, more extra movements, dizziness.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us

## Opicapone (Ongentys) {#opicapone}

- **How to take:** 50 mg once a day at bedtime. **No food for 1 hour before or after.**

- **Side effects:** more extra movements, constipation, trouble sleeping, dizziness.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us

## Tolcapone (Tasmar) {#tolcapone}

- **How to take:** 100 mg 3 times a day. If it is not clearly helping in 3 weeks, we stop it.

- **Liver blood test checklist (required):**

- [ ] Before starting

- [ ] Every 2 to 4 weeks for 6 months

- [ ] Then from time to time

- **Call us right away if:** you have yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, pain in the right upper belly, or unusual tiredness.

- **Side effects:** diarrhea (can be severe), extra movements, orange urine.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us
