# Section 11: Myasthenia gravis and Lambert-Eaton (LEMS)

### About these conditions

Myasthenia gravis and Lambert-Eaton syndrome (LEMS) are **autoimmune** conditions. The immune system makes antibodies that block the signal from the nerve to the muscle. This causes **weakness that gets worse with use and better with rest.**

- **Myasthenia gravis:** droopy eyelids, double vision, and trouble chewing, swallowing, or speaking are common. Arm, leg, and neck weakness can also happen.

- **LEMS:** weakness of the hips and legs is most common. Dry mouth and constipation can also happen. LEMS is sometimes linked to a cancer, so we may check for one.

The medicines in this section help the nerve signal reach the muscle. They treat symptoms, not the cause. Many people also need a medicine that calms the immune system (see **Part C**).

Medicines in this section: pyridostigmine (Mestinon), oxymetazoline eye drops (Upneeq), amifampridine (Firdapse).

**Pregnancy and myasthenia:** most people with myasthenia have healthy pregnancies, but myasthenia can change during pregnancy and after delivery. **Plan pregnancy with us.** Some immune medicines must be stopped months ahead (see A2).

## Oxymetazoline eye drops (Upneeq) for droopy eyelids in myasthenia gravis {#upneeq}

- **What it does:** tightens a small muscle inside the upper eyelid, which lifts the lid. It starts working within minutes and lasts several hours.

- The lift is usually small, about 1 to 2 millimeters. Many people find that enough to see better and look more even.

- It **does not** fix double vision and **does not** treat myasthenia itself. Keep taking your other myasthenia medicines.

- **Good to know:** Upneeq is FDA-approved for droopy eyelids in adults. Using it for myasthenia is "off-label," and insurance may not cover it.

- **How to use:**

  Remove contact lenses first. You can put them back in after **15 minutes**.

  Put **1 drop in each droopy eye, once a day**, in the morning or before the time you most need it.

  One vial is enough for both eyes. **Throw the vial away right after use**, even if some liquid is left.

  Do not let the tip touch your eye or anything else.

  Using other eye drops? Wait **at least 15 minutes** between drops.

- **Using it in only one eye?** In myasthenia, the other eyelid may droop a little more. If that happens, tell us. Using it in both eyes may help.

- **Common side effects:** red eyes, dry eyes, eye irritation or stinging when the drop goes in, blurry vision for a short time, headache. Some people find it works less well over time. Tell us if that happens.

- **Tell us before starting if you have:** glaucoma (especially "narrow-angle"), heart disease, high or low blood pressure that is not controlled, dizziness when standing, a past stroke or poor blood flow, or Sjögren's syndrome.

- **Go to the ER if:** you have eye pain with redness, blurry vision, and sensitivity to light. This can be a rare, sudden rise in eye pressure (angle-closure glaucoma).

- **Call us if:** your eyelids droop more than usual, or you have new double vision or trouble swallowing. These can mean your myasthenia is getting worse. **The drops can hide a droopy eyelid, so do not use them to cover up worsening symptoms.**

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us

## Amifampridine (Firdapse) for LEMS {#amifampridine}

- **How to take:** your total daily dose is split into 3 to 5 doses through the day. Your doctor will raise it by 5 mg a day, every 3 to 4 days, until you are stronger.

- **Never more than 20 mg at one time or 100 mg in a day.**
