# Section 1: Nerve pain (neuropathy)

### About this condition

Peripheral neuropathy means the long nerves that carry feeling to your hands and feet are damaged or not working well. This can cause burning, tingling, numbness, or sharp pain, usually starting in the feet. Common causes include diabetes, low vitamin levels, and some medicines, but sometimes no cause is found. The medicines below calm pain signals from the nerves. They do not repair the nerve, so the goal is **less pain**, not zero pain.

Medicines in this section: gabapentin, pregabalin, duloxetine, low-dose naltrexone, lidocaine (patches and creams), capsaicin (creams and patch).

Also see: Section 2 (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, venlafaxine, which also help nerve pain) and Section 18 (supplements for nerve health: methyl B12, methylfolate, alpha-lipoic acid). **Do not take extra vitamin B6.** Too much B6 can cause nerve damage.

### ❓ "Do gabapentin or pregabalin cause dementia?" {applies=gabapentin,pregabalin}

You may have seen news stories about this. Here is what the research actually shows:

- **No study has shown that these medicines cause dementia.** The studies in the news looked back at medical records. They found that people who took these medicines were diagnosed with dementia a little more often. That is a link, not proof that the medicine caused it.

- **The conditions these medicines treat are linked to memory problems on their own.** Long-term pain, poor sleep, anxiety, depression, diabetes, and high blood pressure all raise the risk of memory problems, whether or not someone takes these medicines. The medicine may simply be a sign that someone has these conditions.

- **When researchers looked only at the best-designed studies, the link was no longer clear.** A 2026 review of all the research concluded that the evidence does not show these medicines cause dementia, and that they should not be avoided when there is a good reason to take them.

- **What is true:** these medicines can make some people feel foggy, sleepy, slow, or unsteady, especially when starting or at higher doses. **This is a side effect, not dementia.** It usually gets better when the dose is lowered.

#### What we do to keep you safe:

- Use the lowest dose that helps your pain.

- Check at your visits whether the medicine is still helping and still needed.

- **Tell us if you feel foggy, forgetful, or unsteady.** We can lower the dose or try something else.

- **Do not stop on your own.** Stopping suddenly can cause trouble sleeping, nausea, anxiety, and sweating. We lower it slowly.

## Lidocaine patches and creams (numbing medicine on the skin) {#lidocaine}

Lidocaine numbs the nerves in the skin where you put it. Very little gets into your blood.

| **Type**                                                                                    | **Strength** | **Prescription?** | **How to use**                                                                       |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ☐ **Lidocaine patch (Lidoderm or generic)**                                                 | 5%           | Yes               | Up to **3 patches at a time** , on for up to **12 hours** , then off for 12 hours.   |
| ☐ **Over-the-counter lidocaine patch** (such as Salonpas Lidocaine or Aspercreme Lidocaine) | 4%           | No                | Follow the package. Usually 1 patch for up to 8 to 12 hours.                         |
| ☐ **Over-the-counter lidocaine cream, gel, or roll-on**                                     | Up to 4%     | No                | Rub a thin layer on the painful area, up to 3 to 4 times a day, as the package says. |

- **Tips:**

- You can cut patches to fit the painful area **before** removing the backing.

- Put on clean, dry, unbroken skin. **Do not use on cuts, rashes, or open wounds.**

- **Do not use a heating pad** over lidocaine. Heat makes your body absorb more.

- Wash your hands after touching it. Keep it away from your eyes.

- **Safety:** used patches still contain medicine. **Fold the sticky sides together** and throw them away where children and pets cannot reach.

- **Common side effects:** mild redness, itching, or rash where it was applied.

- **Stop and call us if:** you feel lightheaded, have a slow heartbeat, or have ringing in your ears.

- **Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

## Capsaicin creams and patch (hot pepper medicine on the skin) {#capsaicin}

Capsaicin comes from chili peppers. With regular use, it "wears out" the pain nerves in the skin so they send fewer pain signals.

| **Type**                                                                       | **Strength**   | **Prescription?**              | **How to use**                                                                                                     |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ☐ **Over-the-counter capsaicin cream or roll-on** (such as Zostrix or generic) | 0.025% to 0.1% | No                             | Rub a thin layer on the painful area **3 to 4 times a day, every day.**                                            |
| ☐ **Qutenza patch**                                                            | 8%             | Yes, applied **in our clinic** | Applied after numbing cream for 30 minutes (feet) or 60 minutes (other areas). Can be repeated **every 3 months.** |

- **Over-the-counter cream tips:**

- **Expect burning or stinging at first.** It is strongest in the first 1 to 2 weeks and fades with regular use. **It only works if you use it every day**, usually for 2 to 4 weeks before you notice a benefit.

- **Wash your hands well right after use**, or wear gloves. If it is on your hands, wait 30 minutes and then wash.

- **Keep it away from your eyes, nose, mouth, and genitals.** It burns badly.

- Do not use on broken skin. Do not use a heating pad or take a hot shower right after.

- If burning is too strong, a cool (not ice-cold) pack can help. Wiping with vegetable oil removes extra cream better than water.

- **Qutenza patch:** burning and redness during and for a few days after are common. You may get cold packs or pain medicine in clinic. Your blood pressure may go up a little during the treatment; we will check it.

- **Breastfeeding:** do not put capsaicin on or near the breast.

- **Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK
