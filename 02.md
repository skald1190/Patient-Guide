# Section 2: Migraine, vestibular migraine, and high brain fluid pressure (IIH)

### About these conditions

**Migraine** is a common brain condition that causes repeated headaches. They are often throbbing, on one side, and come with nausea or sensitivity to light and sound. Some people see flashing lights or zigzag lines before the headache (aura). Migraine cannot be cured, but the right plan can make attacks fewer, shorter, and milder. **Preventive medicines** are taken every day or every month to lower the number of attacks. **As-needed medicines** treat an attack once it starts.

**Vestibular migraine** is a type of migraine where the main symptom is **dizziness or vertigo** (a spinning or rocking feeling), with or without a headache. It is treated with the same lifestyle steps and many of the same preventive medicines as migraine, such as amitriptyline, nortriptyline, venlafaxine, propranolol, verapamil, and topiramate.

**IIH (idiopathic intracranial hypertension)** means the pressure of the fluid around the brain is too high. It can cause daily headaches, a whooshing sound in the ears, and swelling of the nerves at the back of the eyes. If not treated, this swelling can harm your vision. Treatment lowers the fluid pressure, and **regular eye exams are essential** to protect your sight.

Medicines and treatments in this section: propranolol, amitriptyline, nortriptyline, venlafaxine, verapamil, topiramate, CGRP shots (Aimovig, Ajovy, Emgality), gepants (Ubrelvy, Nurtec ODT, Qulipta), Botox, migraine devices (Cefaly, gammaCore, Nerivio), acetazolamide (for IIH).

Also see: Section 18 (supplements for migraine prevention: magnesium glycinate, riboflavin, CoQ10).

**Rule for all migraine patients:** **use as-needed headache medicines fewer than 10 days a month.** Using them more often can cause rebound headaches. Keep a headache diary.

**Pregnancy and migraine:** many migraine medicines are not recommended in pregnancy. **If you are planning a pregnancy, tell us early.** Some medicines (such as CGRP shots) must be stopped months before trying.

## Botox for chronic migraine {#botox-migraine}

- **What happens:** 31 small injections around the forehead, sides of the head, back of the head, neck, and shoulders, **every 12 weeks.** We judge whether it works after 2 treatments.

- **Common side effects:** neck pain or stiffness, a droopy eyelid or eyebrow for a few weeks, soreness where injected.

- **Go to the ER if:** you have trouble swallowing, speaking, or breathing.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK
