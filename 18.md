# Section 18: Supplements (vitamins and nutraceuticals)

### About supplements

Supplements are vitamins, minerals, and natural compounds you can buy without a prescription. Some can help with nerve pain, migraine, muscle problems, and memory. The benefit is usually **modest**, and they work best **alongside** your prescribed medicines, not instead of them.

- [ ] **Start with one multivitamin a day.** A regular once-daily men's or women's multivitamin is all you need as a base. Then add any of the supplements below if you wish.

- **Pregnant or planning a pregnancy?** Take a **prenatal vitamin** instead. It counts as your multivitamin and contains folic acid.

- [ ] **Never take extra vitamin B6 (pyridoxine).** Your multivitamin already has enough. **Extra B6 can cause nerve damage.** Avoid B-complex, "nerve support," and "neuropathy formula" products, which often contain B6. Buy each supplement below on its own and check the label for B6 (pyridoxine or pyridoxal phosphate).

- **They work slowly.** Take each one every day for **about 3 months** before deciding whether it helps.

- **Quality varies between brands.** Your pharmacist can help you choose one.

- **Tell us and your pharmacist about every supplement you take**, just like your medicines.

- **Memory:** B vitamins help most in people with mild memory problems and high homocysteine (a blood test). **No supplement has been proven to prevent dementia.**

## Methyl B12 (methylcobalamin) and methylfolate (L-methylfolate) {#methyl-b12}

- **What it is for:** nerve health and memory. It helps most if your B12 is low or borderline, or your homocysteine is high.

- **Usual dose:** methyl B12 1,500 to 2,000 mcg (micrograms) a day, and methylfolate about 3 mg a day. Some products combine both. My product and dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- [ ] **Blood test before starting:** B12 level (sometimes also methylmalonic acid or homocysteine).

- **Side effects:** uncommon.

- **IMPORTANT: watch out for vitamin B6.** Many "B-complex" and "nerve support" products contain high-dose B6 (pyridoxine). **Too much B6 can cause nerve damage**, even at 50 mg a day taken for months. Check the label. Ask us before taking any product with 50 mg or more of B6.

- **Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

## Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) {#alpha-lipoic-acid}

- **What it is for:** burning, tingling, and numbness from nerve damage. Sometimes used for migraine.

- **Usual dose:** 600 mg once a day. Any form (R-ALA or regular ALA) is fine. My dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- **Side effects:** upset stomach or nausea.

- **Diabetes:** ALA may lower blood sugar a little. If you take diabetes medicine, check your sugar more often for the first few weeks.

## Magnesium glycinate {#magnesium-glycinate}

- **What it is for:** migraine prevention.

- **Usual dose:** 200 to 600 mg a day. My dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- **Side effects:** loose stools or diarrhea. If this happens, lower the dose.

- **Kidney disease? Ask us first.** Magnesium can build up in the blood when the kidneys do not work well.

## Riboflavin (vitamin B2) {#riboflavin}

- **What it is for:** migraine prevention.

- **Usual dose:** 400 mg a day. My dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- **Your urine will turn bright yellow or orange.** This is harmless.

- **Side effects:** rarely, diarrhea or peeing more.

- **Do not try to get 400 mg from a multivitamin.** You would get too much of the other vitamins. Buy riboflavin by itself.

## CoQ10 (coenzyme Q10) {#coq10}

- **What it is for:** migraine prevention, and muscle problems (myopathy), including muscle aches from cholesterol medicines (statins). Results in research are mixed. Some people feel a benefit and others do not.

- **Usual dose:** 100 to 400 mg a day. My dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- **How to take:** with a meal that has some fat, which helps your body absorb it. Take it earlier in the day if it affects your sleep.

- **Side effects:** upset stomach, nausea, trouble sleeping.

- **Good to know:**

- **Taking warfarin (Coumadin)?** Tell us. CoQ10 may make warfarin work less well.

- CoQ10 may lower blood pressure and blood sugar a little.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us
