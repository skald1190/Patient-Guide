# Section 14: Blood pressure drops and POTS (dysautonomia)

### About these conditions

**Dysautonomia** means the "automatic" part of the nervous system is not working well. This part controls blood pressure, heart rate, sweating, digestion, and the bladder.

- **Orthostatic hypotension:** blood pressure **drops** when you stand up, causing dizziness, blurry vision, tiredness, or fainting. It is common with Parkinson's disease, diabetes, and some medicines, and in older adults.

- **POTS (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome):** the **heart races** when you stand up, often with lightheadedness, shakiness, brain fog, and tiredness. Blood pressure usually stays normal. It is most common in younger people and can follow a viral illness.

**Fluids, salt, compression, and movement are the most important treatment for both.** Medicines are added when these are not enough.

### Ways to reach your salt goal:

- **Read food labels.** Add up the "sodium" milligrams (mg) you eat each day.

- **1 teaspoon of table salt = about 2,300 mg sodium.** ½ teaspoon = about 1,150 mg.

- **Salty foods:** broth or bouillon, pickles, olives, salted nuts, pretzels, crackers, cheese, soups, and tomato or vegetable juice.

- **Salt tablets:** a 1-gram salt tablet has about **400 mg sodium.** Take them with a full glass of water and food to avoid stomach upset. My salt tablets: \_\_\_\_\_\_ tablets, \_\_\_\_\_\_ times a day.

- **Electrolyte drinks or powders:** sodium varies a lot between brands. **Check the label.** Many sports drinks are high in sugar and low in sodium.

### Quick fix: the water "bolus"

- Drink **2 cups (16 oz / 500 mL) of plain water quickly, within about 5 minutes.**

- It starts raising blood pressure within a few minutes, works best around 30 minutes later, and lasts about 1 hour.

- **Best times:** before getting out of bed in the morning, before a long time standing, and before showering.

- **Use plain water.** Drinks with sugar or salt do not work as well for this.

### Everyday steps (check the ones we recommend)

☐ **Stand up slowly**, in stages: sit on the edge of the bed for a minute, then stand. Hold onto something.

☐ **Compression:** waist-high compression stockings or tights (30 to 40 mmHg) or an abdominal binder. **Knee-high socks are not enough.** Put them on before getting out of bed.

☐ **Counter-moves when you feel dizzy:** cross your legs and squeeze your thighs and buttocks, squat, clench your fists, or put one foot up on a chair. If you feel faint, **sit or lie down right away.**

☐ **Exercise:** start with exercises **lying down or sitting** (recumbent bike, rowing machine, swimming). Slowly add upright exercise over about 3 months. Expect to feel worse at first; it gets better with steady practice. My plan: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

☐ **Eat smaller meals more often.** Large meals, especially high-carbohydrate meals, can drop blood pressure.

☐ **Avoid heat:** hot showers, hot tubs, saunas, and long periods in the sun can make symptoms worse.

☐ **Raise the head of your bed 4 to 6 inches** (orthostatic hypotension, especially if your blood pressure is high when lying down). Use blocks under the bed legs or a wedge, not just pillows.

☐ **Medicine review:** some medicines (such as blood pressure pills, water pills, and some bladder and Parkinson's medicines) can make blood pressure drops worse. Bring all your medicines to your visit.

☐ **POTS and flushing or hives:** if your episodes also come with flushing, hives, itching, wheezing, or stomach cramps, tell us. Some people with POTS also have a mast cell problem. We may check a blood test (tryptase) and refer you to an allergy specialist.

**Fluids and salt in pregnancy:** POTS and blood pressure drops can change during pregnancy. Do not change your salt or fluid plan without talking to us and your obstetrician.
