# Section 9: Muscle stiffness (spasticity), cramps, and myotonia

### About these conditions

- **Spasticity** is muscle tightness and stiffness caused by damage to the brain or spinal cord, as in MS, stroke, ALS, or spinal cord injury. It can cause stiff legs, painful spasms, and trouble walking.

- **Cramps** are sudden, painful tightening of a muscle that comes and goes. They are common with nerve and muscle conditions.

- **Myotonia** means muscles are slow to relax after you use them, like having trouble letting go after a handshake. It happens in myotonic dystrophy and some other inherited muscle conditions.

Stretching and physical therapy help all three and work best together with medicine.

Medicines in this section: baclofen, tizanidine, methocarbamol, cyclobenzaprine, verapamil, diltiazem, mexiletine.

For people with MS, ALS, spinal cord problems, stroke, neuropathy, or myotonia.

## Mexiletine (for cramps or myotonia) {#mexiletine}

- **How to take:** **always with food.** My dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- **Before starting:** an ECG (heart tracing). If you have myotonic dystrophy, we will also check with cardiology.

- **Common side effects:** heartburn or upset stomach (most common), shakiness, dizziness, headache.

- **Call us right away if:** you faint, or have a racing or pounding heartbeat or chest pain. Also call for a rash with fever or swollen glands, yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, easy bruising, or frequent infections.

- **Good to know:** this is a heart rhythm medicine that we use for muscles. Do not take antacids or baking soda in large amounts without asking.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us
