# "SEEDS" for success

Use these lifestyle steps to lower how often and how badly your headaches happen.

## Sleep

- Keep a consistent sleep schedule. Get up at the same time every day, even on weekends or during vacations.
- Set a bedtime that is early enough for you to get at least 7 hours of sleep.
- Don't go to bed unless you are sleepy.
- If you don't fall asleep after 20 minutes, get out of bed.
- Establish a relaxing bedtime routine.
- Use your bed only for sleep and sex.
- Make your bedroom quiet and relaxing. Keep the room at a comfortable, cool temperature.
- Limit exposure to bright light in the evenings.
- Turn off electronic devices at least 30 minutes before bedtime.
- Don't eat a large meal before bedtime. If you are hungry at night, eat a light, healthy snack.
- Exercise regularly and maintain a healthy diet. Avoid strenuous exercise before bed. A light walk a few hours before bed is okay.
- Avoid consuming caffeine in the late afternoon or evening.
- Avoid consuming alcohol before bedtime.
- Reduce your fluid intake before bedtime.
- Try reading or listening to a podcast before bed. I would recommend the "Nothing Much Happens" podcast.
- Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for insomnia (CBT-I): [veterantraining.va.gov/insomnia](https://www.veterantraining.va.gov/insomnia/)

## Exercise

- Start exercising with a goal of 30 to 60 minutes, 3 to 5 times a week.
- Start small (even 5 minutes once a week!).
- Slowly increase exercise duration and frequency to make it a habit. Choose an activity you enjoy, since no one type of exercise is best.
- If headaches are limiting activity, talk to your doctor about preventive treatment and optimizing your rescue treatment.

## Eat

- There is no single "migraine diet".
- Eat healthy, well-balanced meals at least 3 times a day.
- Avoid skipping meals.
- Recommended fluid intake: about 15.5 cups (3.7 liters, about 125 oz) of fluids a day for men. About 11.5 cups (2.7 liters, about 90 oz) of fluids a day for women.
- Stop caffeine or consistently use less than 200 mg a day (1 to 2 cups of brewed coffee) because of the risk of overuse or rebound headache.
- Aim for optimal weight (body mass index 18.5 to 25 kg/m²).

## Diary

- Track your headaches in a migraine diary. Use a calendar, agenda, or app.
- Bring the diary to your follow-up for your doctor to review.
- A simple paper "stoplight diary":
  - **Red days** = stuck in bed
  - **Yellow days** = headache affects your ability to do your normal day-to-day activities
  - **Green days** = function is not affected
  - **Blank** = 100% headache-free (not even a mild headache)
  - **Checkmark** = needed a rescue medication
- Rescue medications should be used fewer than 2 days per week to avoid a rebound headache called "medication-overuse headache".

## Stress

Migraine is a genetic disorder of dysfunctional sensory processing with headache as a key feature. But your mood and stress can make it worse. To help manage migraine, consider learning the following techniques from books, apps, or a psychologist:

- Cognitive behavioral therapy
- Mindfulness
- Biofeedback
- Relaxation techniques like visualization or progressive muscle relaxation

## Supplements that can help prevent migraine

- Magnesium glycinate 400 mg
- Riboflavin 400 mg
- CoQ10 100 mg
- If you choose to take supplements, please let me know which ones you choose.

## Important considerations for rescue medicines

- You can add acetaminophen 650 mg or an NSAID to step 1 or step 2, but not to both. Do not use them if you have a medical condition that does not allow acetaminophen or NSAIDs.
- Please do not use NSAIDs more than 15 times in one month because of the risk of medication overuse or rebound headache. Examples of NSAIDs are ibuprofen, naproxen, and meloxicam.
- Please do not use acetaminophen more than 15 times in one month because of the risk of medication overuse or rebound headache.
- You can add Benadryl 25 to 50 mg to step 1 or step 2, but not to both.
- Please do not use triptans more than 9 times in one month because of the risk of medication overuse or rebound headache.
- For nausea, you can try ginger tea or ginger candies. You could also try smelling an isopropyl alcohol swab.
