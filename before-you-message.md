# Before you message us

### Check first:

- [ ] Did I check my step-up calendar in my section of this guide?

- [ ] Is this a common side effect listed in my section that may fade with time?

- [ ] Is this an emergency? If so, call 911 instead of messaging.

- [ ] Did I ask my pharmacist about a new medicine interaction? (See **A3**.)

- [ ] Pregnant, planning, or breastfeeding? Did I check the label in A2 and my medicine's pregnancy line?

- [ ] Worried that gabapentin or pregabalin causes dementia? See the box in **Section 1**.

- [ ] Worried that benzodiazepines (like diazepam or clonazepam) or opioids (like methadone or tramadol) cause dementia? See the boxes in **Section 17**.

- [ ] Worried that a bladder medicine (such as oxybutynin) affects memory? See the box in **Section 15**.

### These are common and usually expected. They do not need a message unless they are severe or do not improve:

- Prednisone: bigger appetite, trouble sleeping, mood changes, higher blood sugar

- Flushing or stomach upset during the first month of Tecfidera or Vumerity

- Redness or soreness where you inject

- Mild headache or tiredness after IVIG

- Fever, chills, or aches after the first Kesimpta shot

- Burning from capsaicin cream in the first 1 to 2 weeks

- Dark urine with methocarbamol, orange urine with entacapone, or bright yellow urine with riboflavin

- Tingling in fingers and toes with topiramate or acetazolamide

### What to include in a message:

- Your medicine and dose

- What the symptom is and when it started

- Your temperature, if you feel sick

- Your blood pressure and heart rate (lying and standing), if your question is about dizziness or POTS

**Request refills at least 1 week before you run out.** This is especially important for baclofen, clonidine, guanfacine, seizure medicines, and benzodiazepines, which should never be stopped suddenly.

If your question is still not answered, we are happy to help.
