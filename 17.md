# Section 17: Benzodiazepines and opioids

### About these medicines

- **Benzodiazepines** (clonazepam, diazepam, temazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam, and Nayzilam nasal spray) calm overactive nerves and muscles. In neurology we use them to stop a long seizure, to ease muscle cramps, stiffness, and spasms, and sometimes for short-term sleep problems.

- **Opioids** (methadone, tramadol, oxycodone, buprenorphine, and others) are strong medicines that act on the brain and spinal cord. In our clinic we mostly use them **at low doses for severe restless legs** that iron and other medicines have not controlled. Restless legs experts at Mayo Clinic recommend this. **Restless legs doses are much lower than the doses used for pain.**

Medicines in this section: diazepam (Valium), methadone, tramadol, and other opioids.

### Benzodiazepine safety (17A) {applies=diazepam,diazepam-17,clonazepam,nayzilam,temazepam}

#### Rules for all benzodiazepines

- [ ] **Never mix with opioids or alcohol** unless we planned it together. This combination can stop your breathing. If you take both, keep naloxone (Narcan) at home (see 17B).

- [ ] **These medicines can be habit-forming.** Take only as prescribed.

- [ ] **Never stop suddenly** after regular use. Withdrawal can cause seizures. We will lower the dose slowly.

- [ ] **Older adults:** higher risk of falls, broken bones, confusion, and memory problems.

- [ ] **Do not drive** until you know how it affects you.

- [ ] **Store it locked up and never share it.**

- [ ] **Refills:** early refills cannot be given. Plan ahead.

- [ ] **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (regular use late in pregnancy can make a newborn sleepy or cause withdrawal) · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (watch the baby for sleepiness and poor feeding)

#### Do benzodiazepines cause dementia?

Many people ask us this after reading news stories. **The short answer: some studies have found a link, but it has not been proven that these medicines cause dementia.**

#### Why a link does not mean the medicine is the cause:

- **The reasons people take these medicines can be early signs of memory problems.** Anxiety, poor sleep, and low mood can start years before dementia is diagnosed. In some cases, the early dementia leads to the prescription, not the other way around.

- **When researchers account for this, the link gets much smaller or goes away.** In a large study that followed more than 5,000 adults for about 11 years, benzodiazepine use was not linked to a higher risk of dementia.

- **There is no clear "more medicine, more risk" pattern.** In several large studies, people who took the most did not have more dementia than people who took a little.

**What is proven:** these medicines raise the risk of **falls, broken hips, confusion, and next-day sleepiness**, especially after age 65. Daily use can also cause **dependence**. These are the real reasons we keep the dose low.

#### What we do to keep you safe:

- Use the lowest dose for the shortest time that works, and review it at every visit.

- If you are 65 or older, we look for other options first when we can.

- **Do not stop on your own.** Stopping suddenly after regular use can cause withdrawal, including seizures.

- If we decide to lower it, we go slowly, usually a small step every 2 to 4 weeks. It can take months, and that is normal. We can pause if withdrawal symptoms bother you.

### Opioid safety (17B) {applies=methadone,tramadol,other-opioids,low-dose-opioid}

#### Rules for all opioids

- [ ] **Take exactly as prescribed.** Never take extra for pain, sleep, or a bad night. Never change your dose on your own.

- [ ] **Never mix with alcohol, sleeping pills, or other sedating medicines** unless we planned it together. **Benzodiazepines** (17A) and **gabapentin or pregabalin** (Section 1) add to the risk of slowed breathing. If you take any of these with an opioid, we use lower doses.

- [ ] **Keep naloxone (Narcan) at home.** It is a nasal spray that reverses an opioid overdose. You can buy it without a prescription at any pharmacy, or we can prescribe it. Show your family where it is.

- **Signs of overdose:** cannot be woken up, very slow or shallow breathing, blue or gray lips or fingertips, very small pupils, snoring or gurgling sounds.

- **What to do:** give naloxone, **call 911**, and give a second dose in 2 to 3 minutes if there is no response.

- [ ] **Constipation is the most common side effect.** Start a bowel plan when you start the opioid: drink fluids and take **Miralax (PEG 3350) 17 g every day**. Call us if you have no bowel movement for 3 days.

- [ ] **Snoring or sleep apnea?** Tell us. Opioids can make breathing at night worse. Use your CPAP every night.

- [ ] **Do not drive** until you know how it affects you, and never when sleepy.

- [ ] **Tell every doctor, surgeon, dentist, and ER** that you take an opioid. **Do not take low-dose naltrexone (LDN, Section 1) with an opioid.** It blocks opioids and can cause withdrawal.

- [ ] **Never stop suddenly.** Withdrawal can cause sweating, diarrhea, anxiety, and a bad flare of restless legs. If we stop it, we lower it slowly.

- [ ] **Controlled medicine rules (you will sign an agreement):**

- One prescriber (our office) and one pharmacy for this medicine.

- By Arizona law, we check the state prescription monitoring database before prescribing and regularly after that.

- **No early refills.** Lost or stolen medicine usually cannot be replaced. Plan ahead for travel.

- We may ask for a urine drug test or a pill count. This is routine.

- Never share or sell it. Sharing is dangerous and illegal.

- [ ] **Store it locked up**, away from children, teens, guests, and pets. **One pill can kill a child.** Get rid of unused medicine at a pharmacy drop box.

- [ ] **Pregnant or planning pregnancy?** Tell us right away, and **do not stop on your own.** Babies can be born with opioid withdrawal, so we plan together.

#### Do opioids affect memory or cause dementia?

#### The short answer: this link is less certain than the risks of falls or slowed breathing, but we take it seriously, so we keep doses low.

- Some large studies found that people who used opioids regularly had a somewhat higher chance of memory problems or dementia later.

- **It has not been proven that opioids cause this.** Long-term pain itself affects sleep, mood, and memory, and people who need opioids often have other health problems.

- **The risk seems tied to higher doses and longer use.** In one study, memory decline was seen mainly at high doses, roughly 2 to 3 times the typical restless legs dose.

- **Restless legs doses are low.** In a national registry of people taking opioids for restless legs, most stayed on about the same low dose for 5 years, and their symptoms stayed controlled.

- **Tell us** if you notice new fogginess, memory trouble, sleepiness, or unsteadiness. We may lower the dose.

## Methadone (for restless legs) {#methadone}

- **Why methadone:** it lasts a long time, so one dose can cover the evening and night. In long-term restless legs studies, it rarely stopped working, did not cause augmentation (symptoms getting worse over time), and most people stayed on a steady low dose for years.

## Tramadol (Ultram) {#tramadol}

- **What it is:** a milder opioid that also acts a little like an antidepressant.

- **How to take:** My dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_. When: \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_.

- **Restless legs warning:** tramadol is the one opioid that can cause **augmentation**, like ropinirole: symptoms start earlier in the day, get stronger, or spread to your arms. **Call us if this happens.** Do not take more.

- **Seizures:** tramadol can cause seizures, even at normal doses. The risk is higher if you have epilepsy, or take bupropion (Wellbutrin) or some antidepressants. Tell us if you have ever had a seizure.

- **Serotonin syndrome:** tramadol can react with duloxetine (Cymbalta), venlafaxine (Effexor), SSRIs (like sertraline), amitriptyline, nortriptyline, mirtazapine, cyclobenzaprine, and triptans (migraine medicines). **Go to the ER** for agitation, confusion, fever, heavy sweating, shaking, muscle twitching or stiffness, or diarrhea.

- **Some medicines make tramadol work less well and raise seizure risk:** bupropion, fluoxetine (Prozac), and paroxetine (Paxil). **Carbamazepine should not be taken with tramadol.** See **A3**.

- **Common side effects:** nausea, dizziness, constipation, sleepiness, headache.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (babies can have withdrawal after birth with long-term use) · **Breastfeeding:** ⛔ **Avoid** (some babies can get dangerous levels and slowed breathing)
