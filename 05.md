# Section 5: Seizures

### About this condition

A seizure is a sudden burst of abnormal electrical activity in the brain that briefly mixes up the brain's messages. Depending on where it starts, a seizure can cause staring, confusion, strange feelings or smells, jerking, stiffening, or loss of awareness. **Epilepsy** means having a tendency to have repeated seizures. **Most people with epilepsy can control their seizures with medicine.** Taking your medicine every day, getting enough sleep, and limiting alcohol are the most important things you can do.

Medicines in this section: levetiracetam (Keppra), lacosamide (Vimpat), lamotrigine (Lamictal), cenobamate (Xcopri), clonazepam (Klonopin), Nayzilam rescue spray.

### Rules for all seizure medicines

- [ ] **Never stop suddenly.** This can cause seizures.

- [ ] **Fill out and follow "My Seizure Action Plan" below.** Share it with family, roommates, and coworkers.

- [ ] **Birth control and other medicines:** some seizure medicines change how birth control and other medicines work. See **A3**.

### 1. First aid: what others should do during a seizure (Stay, Safe, Side)

- [ ] **Stay** with the person. **Time the seizure** with a watch or phone.

- [ ] **Safe:** move sharp or hard objects away. Cushion the head. Loosen anything tight around the neck.

- [ ] **Side:** gently turn the person onto their side once the jerking slows or stops, so they can breathe and saliva can drain.

- [ ] **Never put anything in the person's mouth.** Do not hold them down.

- [ ] Stay with them until they are fully awake and know where they are.

### 3. Call 911 if:

- [ ] A seizure lasts **longer than 5 minutes**, or seizures keep coming after rescue medicine

- [ ] A second seizure starts before the person wakes up from the first

- [ ] Breathing is not normal, or lips or face turn blue, after the seizure ends

- [ ] The person is not waking up after the seizure stops

- [ ] The person is injured, the seizure happened in water, or the person is pregnant

- [ ] It is the person's first seizure, or the first time rescue medicine is used

### 4. Staying safe day to day

- [ ] **Showers, not baths**, until your seizures are controlled. Never bathe or swim alone.

- [ ] Swim only with a buddy who knows what to do, and wear a life jacket for boating.

- [ ] No climbing ladders or heights, and no working alone near open flames, hot stoves, or heavy machinery, until seizures are controlled. Cook on back burners and use a microwave when you can.

- [ ] **Common seizure triggers:** missed medicine, too little sleep, alcohol or drugs, illness or fever, and stress. Use a pill box and phone alarms.

### 5. Driving (Arizona)

- [ ] **Arizona law: do not drive until you have been seizure-free for at least 3 months.** This includes seizures where you lose awareness, even if you do not fall or shake.

- [ ] In Arizona, **it is your responsibility to report your seizures to the Arizona MVD.** Your doctor may complete a medical form for you.

- [ ] The 3 months start over after any seizure that affects awareness or control.

- [ ] **Commercial driving (CDL):** federal rules are much stricter. People with epilepsy usually need to be seizure-free for 8 years; after a single unprovoked seizure, usually 4 years. Ask us if this applies to you.

### 6. SUDEP (Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy)

We talk with everyone who has epilepsy about SUDEP. It is rare, but you deserve to know about it, because **there are things you can do to lower the risk.**

- SUDEP happens to about **1 in 1,000 adults with epilepsy each year.** That means **999 out of 1,000 are not affected** each year.

- **The biggest risk is having convulsive seizures** (whole-body stiffening and jerking, also called tonic-clonic seizures), especially if they are frequent or happen during sleep.

- **The risk goes down a lot when these seizures are controlled.**

### How to lower your risk:

- [ ] Take your seizure medicine **every day**, exactly as prescribed.

- [ ] Get enough sleep, and limit alcohol.

- [ ] **Tell us about every convulsive seizure**, even if it seems minor.

- [ ] If you have seizures at night, ask us about seizure alarms or monitoring devices, or having someone nearby at night.

- [ ] **If 2 seizure medicines have not controlled your seizures,** ask us about a referral to an epilepsy center. Options include surgery and devices such as VNS (vagus nerve stimulation).

### 7. Pregnancy and folic acid

- [ ] **Take folic acid every day if you could ever become pregnant**, even if you are not planning a pregnancy right now. It lowers the risk of some birth defects and may help your baby's development. **My folic acid dose: \_\_\_\_\_\_ mg a day** (at least 0.4 mg).

- [ ] **Plan ahead.** Tell us before you try to get pregnant. Most people with epilepsy have healthy pregnancies, and the safest time to adjust medicines is **before** pregnancy.

- [ ] **If you find out you are pregnant, do not stop your seizure medicine.** Call us the same day.

- [ ] Blood levels of some seizure medicines (such as lamotrigine and levetiracetam) **drop during pregnancy**. We check levels and adjust your dose, then lower it again after delivery.

- [ ] **Valproate (Depakote) and topiramate (Topamax) should be avoided in pregnancy** if at all possible.

- [ ] **Breastfeeding** is encouraged on most seizure medicines.

- [ ] You can join the **North American Antiepileptic Drug Pregnancy Registry** to help other families: **https://www.aedpregnancyregistry.org** or **1-888-233-2334**.

- [ ] More information: **MotherToBaby** at https://mothertobaby.org (see A2).

## Lamotrigine (Lamictal) {#lamotrigine}

**Follow your calendar exactly.** Going up too fast raises the risk of a serious rash. A starter kit from the pharmacy can make this easier.

**Why there are different plans:** some medicines change how fast your body clears lamotrigine.

- **Valproate (Depakote) slows it down**, so lamotrigine builds up. You need a **slower, smaller** plan.

- **Carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, and primidone speed it up**, so lamotrigine is cleared fast. You need a **bigger** plan.

- **Birth control with estrogen also speeds it up.** Your goal dose may need to be higher.

#### If another seizure medicine is started or stopped, your lamotrigine dose usually needs to change:

- **Stopping valproate:** we usually **raise** your lamotrigine dose over about 2 weeks.

- **Stopping carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, or primidone:** we usually **lower** your lamotrigine dose over about 2 to 3 weeks.

- **Starting valproate:** we **lower** your lamotrigine dose first.

- **Never start or stop any of these medicines without telling us.**

#### Safety rules for lamotrigine:

- **STOP and call us right away** for any rash, fever, mouth sores, or swollen glands, even a mild rash. **Go to the ER** if the rash is blistering or peeling, or if your eyes or mouth are involved.

- **Missed 5 or more days in a row? Do NOT restart at your usual dose.** Call us. You will likely need to start the calendar over from the beginning.

- **Common side effects:** dizziness, double vision, headache, trouble sleeping.

- **Taking cenobamate (Xcopri) too?** Cenobamate also lowers lamotrigine levels. See the cenobamate section below.

- **Pregnancy:** ✅ Generally OK (one of the lowest-risk seizure medicines; levels drop during pregnancy, so we check them often) · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK

- **Common side effects:** sleepiness, dizziness, tiredness, double vision, unsteadiness. These are more common as the dose goes up. Call us; do not just push through.

## Nayzilam (midazolam nasal spray), seizure rescue {#nayzilam}

#### For family and caregivers:

1.  If a seizure cluster happens as described in your seizure action plan, spray **1 spray into one nostril.**

2.  If seizures continue after **10 minutes**, give **1 more spray into the other nostril.**

3.  **Do not give the second spray** if the person is having trouble breathing or is very hard to wake up.

**Call 911 if:** seizures continue after the second spray, breathing is not normal, or this is the first time using it.

**Limits:** no more than 2 sprays per episode, no more than 1 episode every 3 days, and no more than 5 episodes a month.

**After using it:** expect sleepiness. Do not drive for at least 4 hours. Tell us each time it is used. See **Section 17: Benzodiazepine safety.**

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us (rescue use is usually worth it; a long seizure is risky for the baby) · **Breastfeeding:** ✅ Generally OK for occasional rescue use
