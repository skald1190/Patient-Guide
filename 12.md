# Section 12: Periodic paralysis

### About this condition

Periodic paralysis is a rare **inherited** condition that causes **attacks of muscle weakness or paralysis** lasting minutes to days, with normal or near-normal strength in between. Attacks are linked to potassium shifting in and out of the muscles. They are often triggered by things like a large carbohydrate meal, resting after exercise, stress, or cold. **Knowing your type matters**, because the attack plan is different for each type. Avoiding triggers and taking daily preventive medicine can greatly reduce attacks.

Medicines in this section: potassium chloride (for attacks), acetazolamide (Diamox), dichlorphenamide (Keveyis).

**For the ER team (show them this):** total body potassium is not low. Watch for rebound high potassium. If IV potassium is needed, give it in mannitol. **Avoid glucose and saline IV fluids;** they can worsen weakness.

#### High-potassium type

- Eat small carbohydrate snacks often. Do not skip meals.

- Avoid high-potassium foods (bananas, potatoes, oranges, salt substitutes).

- **Do not take potassium pills.**
