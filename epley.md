# Epley maneuver for positional vertigo (BPPV)

## What causes this kind of vertigo

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is the most common cause of vertigo. Tiny calcium crystals in the inner ear come loose and drift into one of the balance canals. When you move your head (rolling over in bed, looking up, bending down), the crystals shift and cause a brief, strong spinning feeling, often with nausea.

The Epley maneuver is a series of head and body positions that moves the crystals back to where they belong. It works for most people, often after just 1 to 3 rounds. It only helps BPPV, not other causes of dizziness.

## How to do it at home

Your doctor will show you the maneuver first and tell you which ear to treat.

Set up: sit on your bed with a pillow behind you, placed so that when you lie back it will be under your shoulders, not your head.

1. **Turn your head 45 degrees toward the ear we are treating.**
2. **Lie back quickly**, keeping your head turned, so your shoulders are on the pillow and your head tips slightly back over it. Stay here for **30 seconds**, or until the spinning stops.
3. **Without lifting your head, turn it 90 degrees to the other side.** Stay for **30 seconds**.
4. **Roll onto that side**, turning your head a little further so you are looking down at the floor. Stay for **30 seconds**.
5. **Sit up slowly** on that side of the bed. Stay seated for a minute before standing.

Do this **3 times in a row**. Many people like to do it just before bed, so they can sleep through any leftover dizziness. Repeat each night until you have had **24 hours with no positional vertigo**.

## What to expect

- The spinning can feel **stronger during the maneuver**. That is common and usually means the crystals are moving.
- Some people feel nauseated or off-balance for the rest of the day. Avoid quick head movements and bending over for the rest of that day.
- If nausea is a problem, ask us about a medicine to take 30 to 60 minutes before.

## Check with us first if you have

- Neck or back problems or a recent injury
- A detached retina or recent eye surgery
- Narrowed blood vessels in the neck, or a recent stroke

## Call us if

- The vertigo is not better after a week of doing the maneuver.
- You have new symptoms: a new or severe headache, double vision, trouble speaking or swallowing, weakness, numbness, or trouble walking. **Call 911 if these come on suddenly.**
