# Section 15: Bladder problems

### About this condition

**Neurogenic bladder** means bladder problems caused by a nerve condition such as MS, Parkinson's disease, a spinal cord problem, or dysautonomia.

- **Overactive bladder:** the bladder squeezes too often, causing sudden urges, peeing often, leaks, or waking at night to pee.

- **Bladder that does not empty well:** a weak stream, feeling full after peeing, or frequent bladder infections.

Some people have both. The right medicine depends on which problem you have, which is why we check how well your bladder empties first.

Medicines in this section: mirabegron (Myrbetriq), vibegron (Gemtesa), trospium, oxybutynin, solifenacin (Vesicare), tamsulosin (Flomax).

For people with MS, Parkinson's, spinal cord problems, or dysautonomia.

**Before starting:** a urine test to rule out infection and a bladder scan to check how well your bladder empties.

**Give it 4 to 8 weeks** to judge how well it works.

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** there is little safety information for bladder medicines in pregnancy or breastfeeding. If this applies to you, talk to us.

### ❓ "Can bladder medicines like oxybutynin affect my memory or cause dementia?" {applies=oxybutynin,solifenacin,trospium}

This is a fair question. The answer is **different** from gabapentin and pregabalin (Section 1), so we want to be straightforward with you.

- **Some bladder medicines block acetylcholine,** a brain chemical that helps with memory. They are called **anticholinergic** medicines. Oxybutynin, solifenacin (Vesicare), tolterodine (Detrol), and trospium are in this group.

- **Large studies have found a link between these medicines and dementia, mostly with long-term use.** The longer and more often people took them, the higher the risk. The biggest increase was in people who took them every day for about **3 years or more**. Short-term use showed little or no increase.

- **This is still a link, not proof.** But it has shown up in many studies, so bladder experts now recommend using these medicines carefully.

- **If you have taken one of these for a long time, it does not mean you will get dementia.** Most people who took them never do.

- **Not all bladder medicines are the same:**

- **Oxybutynin** reaches the brain the most, especially the short-acting pill. It can also cause short-term fogginess within hours of a dose, especially in older adults.

- **Trospium** reaches the brain very little.

- **Mirabegron (Myrbetriq) and vibegron (Gemtesa)** work in a completely different way and do not block acetylcholine. **Bladder experts recommend trying these first.** In a study of older adults, mirabegron did not change memory test scores.

#### What we do to keep you safe:

- Try mirabegron or vibegron first when possible.

- If an anticholinergic bladder medicine is a better fit for you, we choose one that reaches the brain less (such as trospium), or the long-acting pill or patch form of oxybutynin, at the lowest dose that works.

- **If you already have memory problems or take a memory medicine** (donepezil or rivastigmine, Section 7), we usually avoid these medicines.

- **Other medicines can add to the effect.** Examples are sleep aids and allergy pills with diphenhydramine (Benadryl, Tylenol PM, ZzzQuil) and some older antidepressants. Bring all your medicines to your visit, and ask your pharmacist before buying over-the-counter sleep or allergy pills.

- **Do not stop on your own.** Your bladder symptoms may come back. Ask us about switching instead.

## Tamsulosin (Flomax) (to help the bladder empty) {#tamsulosin}

- **How to take:** 0.4 mg once a day, 30 minutes after the same meal each day.

- **Side effects:** dizziness when standing (stand up slowly), stuffy nose, less fluid with ejaculation.

- **Cataract surgery:** tell your eye surgeon you take this medicine.

- **Pregnancy:** ⚠️ Discuss with us · **Breastfeeding:** ⚠️ Discuss with us
